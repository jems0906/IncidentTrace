# Root cause analysis method guide

IncidentTrace stores an investigator's reasoning; it does not infer, score, or declare root causes. A completed worksheet remains subject to human review and should be supported by the investigation record.

## Start with the event, not a verdict

Write a concise factual account of the event and outcome. Separate what was observed or corroborated from estimates, assumptions, and unresolved questions. Avoid labels about character, attitude, or intent. An unsafe act may describe a point in the sequence, but does not explain why the conditions and controls allowed it.

## Four core questions

1. **What happened?** Describe the event and actual or potential consequence.
2. **How did it happen?** Reconstruct the sequence and conditions using evidence.
3. **Why did it happen?** Examine contributing conditions and underlying management, design, planning, and organizational controls.
4. **What needs to be corrected?** Identify changes that address supported causes and can be verified in practice.

## 5 Whys

Begin with a specific, evidence-supported problem. Ask why the preceding condition existed, and repeat only while each answer is useful and testable. There may be several branches, fewer or more than five questions, and multiple causes. Validate each answer against interviews, records, observations, and procedure. Do not treat the fifth answer as automatically being the root cause. Stop and gather evidence when an answer is speculative.

## Fishbone worksheet

Use these headings as prompts, not as a mandatory checklist or automated classification:

- **Task:** sequence, workload, pace, variability, simultaneous work, and access.
- **Equipment / material:** design, condition, suitability, maintenance, and material variation.
- **Environment:** layout, visibility, noise, lighting, weather, and changing conditions.
- **Supervision:** coordination, staffing, escalation, monitoring, and available support.
- **Training:** task-specific preparation, practice, and demonstrated authorization where required.
- **Procedures:** availability, clarity, usability, safe work design, and how procedures match normal work.

Explore links between categories. Also consider organizational decisions, purchasing, planning, change management, and the design of safeguards. A blank category is acceptable when evidence does not support a contribution.

## Contributing factors

For each selected factor, state the evidence and explain how the condition contributed to the event or weakened a control. Distinguish an underlying cause from a consequence or coincidental condition. Record conflicting evidence and uncertainty. The investigator chooses and justifies every factor.

## Review before approval

A competent reviewer should ask whether the explanation fits all evidence, whether normal work conditions were considered, whether the analysis stopped too early at an individual action, whether alternative explanations were tested, and whether each recommended action addresses a supported system condition. Record reviewer identity and unresolved questions.
