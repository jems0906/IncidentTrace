# Sample investigation: pallet shifted at east dispatch bay

**Fictional training scenario.** Names, site, and records in this example are invented. The analysis below is a documented investigator judgment, not an automated finding.

## Intake

- **Type:** Near miss
- **Date / location:** September 18, 2026, 08:42; east dispatch bay, Northstar Distribution Centre
- **Reported by:** Jordan Lee
- **Outcome:** A pallet shifted approximately 20 cm as it was raised from a floor-level stack. The load stayed on the forks. Nobody was injured.
- **Immediate actions:** The operator stopped and parked the truck with forks lowered. The bay was isolated, adjacent loads were checked, and dispatch paused for a competent load-stability inspection.
- **Reporting frame:** The purpose is to understand how the event was possible and improve work controls, not to assign blame.

## Investigator-selected contributing factors

- **Equipment / material:** Two lower boards were split and pallet footprints were mixed. The underside condition was not visible from the normal pre-use viewing position.
- **Procedures:** The inspection instruction lacked rejection criteria for concealed underside damage and mixed footprints, and did not specify a safe inspection method.
- **Task:** A floor-level lift was attempted before a documented load-stability check for a suspect pallet.
- **Environment:** The lower boards were outside the normal sightline. This is documented as a contributing inspection condition, not as an excuse or a personal failing.

## Human-reviewed root cause worksheet

**What happened?** A pallet shifted approximately 20 cm while being raised at the east dispatch bay. The operator stopped; the load remained on the forks and nobody was injured.

**How did it happen?** The pallet had split lower boards and mixed footprints. It moved laterally as the forks took the load.

**Why did it happen?** The damage was on the underside, outside the normal pre-use sightline. The inspection procedure did not define rejection criteria for concealed board damage or mixed footprints, and there was no separate stability check for suspect loads.

**What needs to be corrected?** Revise pallet acceptance criteria and safe inspection method; quarantine damaged pallets; introduce a pre-lift stability check for suspect loads; verify the revised controls in the dispatch bay.

**5 Whys worksheet:** The pallet shifted; damaged lower boards and mixed footprints reduced support; underside damage was not identified; routine viewing and procedure did not expose or define the defect; pallet acceptance and dispatch planning did not provide clear criteria and verification. The fifth answer is a working analysis, not a formulaic declaration.

**Fishbone prompts reviewed:** Task (no documented stability pause for suspect pallet); equipment/material (split boards, mixed footprints); environment (underside outside normal sightline); supervision (no dispatch pause point); training (no recorded refresher on concealed defect criteria); procedures (criteria and method undefined).

## Preventive action and effectiveness review

**Action:** Revise pallet acceptance criteria and verify checks at dispatch.  
**Linked finding:** Acceptance criteria did not cover concealed underside board damage or mixed footprints.  
**Owner:** Evan Brooks. **Due:** September 25, 2026.  
**Interim controls:** Quarantine damaged pallets; use a two-person underside check for suspect loads; supervisor verifies the shift check log.

**Effectiveness review (October 2, 2026):** Priya Shah, independent of the action owner, sampled 24 pallet checks across three shifts. Rejection criteria were applied consistently, damaged pallets were quarantined, and no pallet movement or recurrence was observed during the review period. Monthly sampling will continue for one quarter. The action was closed after this documented review.

## Toolbox talk and learning shared

**Talk:** “Stable loads: pause, check, and reset,” facilitated by Priya Shah on September 19, 2026.

**Briefing:** Check pallet condition and load stability before lifting; use the marked exclusion zone; stop and lower the load if it shifts; isolate and report damaged pallets. Crew prompt: “What conditions would make you pause a lift?”

**Attendance:** Jordan Lee, Mina Patel, Evan Brooks, Luis Romero, and Sam Chen. The record documents attendance only; it does not certify demonstrated competency.

## Lessons learned

- Design acceptance checks around the actual visibility and condition of equipment and materials.
- Define actionable rejection criteria, a safe inspection method, and a clear escalation path.
- Provide interim controls while system changes are being implemented.
- Verify controls in representative work across shifts; completion alone is not evidence of effectiveness.
- Share learning with workers and keep attendance distinct from competency evidence.
