# Incident investigation procedure

**Document owner:** Safety and Operations  
**Applies to:** Injury, vehicle incident, property damage, near miss, and environmental events  
**Purpose:** Learn from events and near misses, correct underlying system conditions, and prevent recurrence. This procedure is not a process for assigning blame.

## 1. Make the scene safe

Respond to immediate danger, provide emergency assistance, and stop or isolate affected work where needed. Preserve the scene when it is safe and practical to do so. Record what was done before investigation began, who made the decision, and when. Statutory notifications and evidence-preservation duties must be handled under applicable local requirements; this procedure does not replace them.

## 2. Report promptly

Any worker may report an injury, vehicle incident, property damage, near miss, or environmental event. Record the time, location, people involved or witnessing, a factual description, and immediate actions. Use neutral, observable language. Do not delay a report while deciding whether an event is serious enough to investigate. Protect sensitive personal information and restrict access as required by organizational policy and law.

## 3. Appoint the investigator and set scope

A responsible manager assigns a competent investigator and sets proportionate scope based on actual and potential consequences, recurrence potential, and complexity. Include workers with relevant task knowledge. The investigator records assumptions, uncertainties, and the review date. Investigation is intended to identify conditions and controls, not to determine fault.

## 4. Collect and preserve evidence

Collect evidence as soon as practical, without creating additional risk. Consider scene photographs or sketches, equipment and material condition, relevant records, work instructions, training records, maintenance, schedules, environmental conditions, and interviews. Record source, date, collector, context, and a link or secure reference. Separate direct observation from recollection and inference. Do not alter source records; document corrections and preserve originals under the applicable retention policy.

Interview people privately and respectfully. Explain the purpose, ask open questions, establish sequence and context, and check understanding. Do not lead witnesses toward a preferred explanation. Give participants an opportunity to clarify factual notes. Record disagreements or gaps rather than forcing consensus.

## 5. Build the event timeline

Record significant events before, during, and after the incident in chronological order. For each entry, identify the source and distinguish confirmed fact from estimate. Compare accounts and records; note time-zone or clock differences. A timeline is an evidence index and sequence aid, not proof of causation by itself.

## 6. Identify contributing conditions

Consider the task, equipment and materials, environment, supervision, training, and procedures. Select only factors supported by evidence and explain the rationale. Include interactions between conditions and normal work practices. Avoid stopping at an individual's action: ask what conditions made the action likely, possible, or difficult to detect, and what barriers were absent or ineffective.

## 7. Document root cause reasoning

Use the four prompts: **What happened? How did it happen? Why did it happen? What needs to be corrected?** Use 5 Whys as iterative questioning, not as a quota or proof. Use the fishbone worksheet to explore task, equipment/material, environment, supervision, training, and procedures. Consider management, design, planning, and organizational controls. Do not label a person "careless" as a root cause. Record evidence, uncertainty, investigator, and reviewer. Root-cause conclusions are human judgments and must be reviewed by a competent person.

## 8. Select and track preventive actions

Prefer controls that address the supported system condition and are practical in the real work context. Assign an accountable owner and due date. Define interim controls for the period before completion. Link each action to the finding it addresses. Record completion evidence. An action is not closed merely because work was performed or a document was revised.

## 9. Verify effectiveness independently

After implementation, a reviewer distinct from the action owner evaluates whether the control was implemented and works under representative conditions. Define the review method and sample before the review where practical. Record evidence, recurrence or leading-indicator observations, limitations, and follow-up date. If the control is ineffective or evidence is insufficient, keep the action open or reopen it, revise the action, and set another review. Only close the action after the independent reviewer records the effectiveness result.

## 10. Share learning and retain the record

Prepare a concise summary of the event, evidence timeline, contributing factors, investigator-reviewed reasoning, actions, and lessons. Share only information appropriate to the audience. Toolbox talks should invite practical questions and explain changed controls. Record facilitator, delivery date, attendees, and an understanding-check prompt. Attendance is an attendance record; it is not proof of competency. Keep records, personal data, attachments, and access controls according to organizational retention and privacy requirements.

## 11. Review and improve

The responsible manager checks that immediate controls, investigation quality, actions, independent reviews, and learning delivery are complete. Review trends and overdue or ineffective actions periodically. Update procedures and risk controls when evidence indicates a broader issue.

## Record quality check

- Incident type, date, location, factual description, reporter, and immediate actions are recorded.
- Timeline entries have dates and traceable sources.
- Evidence sources and uncertainties are noted.
- Factors are investigator-selected and justified.
- RCA answers the four questions and is human-reviewed.
- Actions connect to findings and name owner, due date, and interim controls.
- Effectiveness is evidenced and reviewed by someone other than the owner before closure.
- Toolbox attendance and understanding discussion are recorded without representing attendance as demonstrated competence.
